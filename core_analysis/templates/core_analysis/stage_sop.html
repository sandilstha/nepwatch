Stage Analysis Desk SOP

Methodology note for the Stage Analysis desk (Technical Analysis tab). It adapts Stan Weinstein's four-stage cycle to daily NEPSE data to time short-term swing entries and exits: buy strength as it emerges from a base, avoid tops, and step aside in decline. It is a rules-based screen, not investment advice.

1. What the desk does

The desk classifies a single symbol's current position in the Weinstein cycle from its own price and volume history, then reports the live stage, how long it has held, the confirmation score, and the last stage change. The read is anchored on two moving averages rescaled from Weinstein's weekly chart to daily bars:

  • 30-week baseline — a 150-day EMA (ema_30w). Price above a rising baseline is the core requirement for an advance.
  • 10-week trigger — a 50-day EMA (ema_10w). The faster line that price rolls under first when a trend tops out.

Adjusted prices are used first; missing dates fall back to unadjusted and are flagged in the Source column.

2. The four stages

StageCharacterShort-term stance
Stage 1 BaseSideways after a decline; 30w MA flattening, no trend.Watch. Build a watchlist; no position yet.
Stage 2 AdvancePrice above a rising 30w MA, breaking resistance on volume.Buy / hold. This is the only stage the desk actively favours for longs.
Stage 3 Top30w MA has flattened; price has rolled below the 10w MA but not yet decisively below the 30w baseline.Trim / tighten stops. New buys off the table.
Stage 4 DeclinePrice below a falling 30w MA.Avoid / exit. No longs; wait for a fresh Stage 1 base.
When more than one condition set matches, the desk resolves ties by priority: Stage 2 > Stage 3 > Stage 4 > Stage 1. The MA slope is split into rising / flat / falling around a ±0.2% five-bar band so a top (flat) and a decline (falling) never both fire.

Stage 2 requires all of: above baseline, MA rising, breakout, volume confirm, and momentum confirm (plus RSI/ADX if those gates are enabled). The score is informational — a 5/7 or 6/7 flags a near-miss worth watching even when the strict Stage 2 label has not yet triggered.

5. Short-term playbook

Weinstein is a medium-term framework; on daily bars this desk surfaces short-term swing timing. Trade the transition, not the label alone:

  • Entry — fresh Stage 2. Favour a Stage 1 → Stage 2 transition with a low bars-in-stage count and a high score (6/7 or 7/7). A brand-new breakout has the most room; a Stage 2 that is 60+ bars old and far above the 30w EMA (large % from 30w EMA) is extended — a worse short-term entry.
  • Exit — Stage 3 or 3→4. Treat the first Stage 3 print, or a Stage 3 → Stage 4 transition, as the signal to trim and tighten stops. The desk deliberately marks the top when the baseline flattens rather than waiting for a full breakdown.
  • Stand aside — Stage 4 / Stage 1. No longs. Add Stage 1 names to a watchlist and wait for the next Stage 2.
  • Confirm with volume. A breakout with volume ratio below 1.5× is suspect; for short-term trades weight the volume-confirm flag heavily.
  • Respect provisional reads. On a new listing the stage is based on a shortened baseline — size smaller and re-check once ~150 days exist.

6. Operating steps

  • Open the Technical Analysis tab, search and select the exact symbol.
  • Set a 1Y+ range (use the 1Y / 2Y / 3Y quick-range buttons) so the 150-day baseline is real.
  • Leave parameters at defaults for a first pass; enable the RSI and/or ADX gate only when you want a stricter Stage 2.
  • Execute the Stage scan. Read the live stage, action, score/7, bars-in-stage, % from 30w EMA, and the last transition.
  • Cross-check the breakout bar's volume and the data-source note before acting.

7. Limits

The read is only as good as the synced EOD history. Thin trading, flat/illiquid bars, gaps, and stale prices distort the moving averages and the volume ratio. Stages can whipsaw around the flat band in choppy markets, and a provisional new-listing read can change materially once a full baseline exists. Parameters are tunable, so two operators can get different labels on the same symbol.

This is a descriptive timing aid. It does not forecast price, guarantee a breakout follows through, or replace risk management and position sizing.
