Stock 360 — where every number comes from

One symbol in, every desk's read on it out. This page says which table or service each panel is reading, in short.

1. The one rule that explains most of the page

Adjusted prices for anything measured over time; the raw exchange print for "what it did today." A bonus or split makes the price fall without anyone selling, so a return calculated on raw closes would invent a crash. Everything that spans days — returns, volatility, moving averages, RSI, beta, the SOP backtest — runs on the split/bonus-adjusted series. The hero quote (last close, day change, volume, 52-week range, market cap) is the raw print, because that is the number the exchange actually published.

The adjusted series is built once per request and shared by every block that needs it, so the returns, the technicals and the SOP signal can never disagree about the price history.

3. Two sources of fundamentals, and which one wins

The ratio cards and the statement matrix read the platform's own financial-statement table (fundamentals_financialstatdbs), harvested by the separate fundamentals app. The dividend card and the valuation workspace read a second store, FundaFundamentalSnapshot — a per-symbol snapshot synced on demand from funda.aurasrp.com.np. A synced snapshot is preferred over a live read; a symbol nobody has synced simply shows no dividend history rather than a guess.

4. Mutual funds are a different instrument

A mutual fund files no balance sheet and no income statement — it has no revenue and no ROE — so P/E, P/B and EPS are category errors for one, and every ratio card on this page is structurally blank for a fund no matter how often the fundamentals sync runs. What a fund publishes instead is its NAV (net asset value per unit).

Because almost every Nepali scheme is closed-end, the figure that matters is the gap between NAV and the market price: a fund trading at 8.96 against a NAV of 9.93 is on a 9.8% discount. That is the fund equivalent of P/B, and it is what the Fund NAV & discount panel leads with. A negative number means trading below NAV, which is the normal state here.

NAV is a month-end reading, not a live one. Funds report per Nepali month (e.g. "Asadh 2083"), so the discount pairs a current market price against a NAV that may be up to a month old. Open-end schemes also publish a daily NAV, which the panel shows when it exists. The panel states our own close-vs-NAV figure and, alongside it, the publisher's own figure from its own price snapshot — so a disagreement is explainable rather than mysterious.

5. What is computed here vs. borrowed from another desk

  • Computed on this page: the quote, returns, volatility and every technical reading. They are server-rendered so the page cannot show a figure that no endpoint would confirm.
  • Borrowed, deliberately: the SOP signal runs the Strategy Simulator's engine with that desk's own defaults, and the floorsheet block calls the broker desk's endpoint. Neither has a private copy of the logic, so Stock 360 and the desk it links to cannot print different calls for the same symbol.
  • Linked, not embedded: the full valuation model and the seller→buyer flow map live on their own pages; the panels here link across rather than keep a thinner duplicate.

6. Freshness and caching

  • The computed payload is cached per (symbol, latest EOD date) for 30 minutes, so the pandas work runs once per symbol per session, not on every page view. A new trading session invalidates it automatically.
  • The AI narrative is cached for ~6 hours — one Gemini spend per symbol per trading day, roughly.
  • The freshness chip in the hero answers two different questions with one label: is the platform's EOD store up to date at all, and has this symbol traded since. A stock that simply did not trade is not a broken pipeline, and the chip says which it is.

7. Limits, said plainly

No ATR, ADX or DMI anywhere on this page. A large share of NEPSE bars print high equal to low. Range-based indicators divide by that range, so on this market they produce confident-looking nonsense. They are left out rather than shown with a caveat.

  • Adjustments can go stale. If the corporate-action table has not been synced, the "adjusted" series quietly degrades toward raw closes, and returns spanning a bonus issue will be wrong. The freshness chip covers price staleness, not adjustment staleness.
  • Dividends come from what was filed. The fiscal year's payout is taken as the largest DividendPerShare reported in that year — Q4 usually carries it, but a company reporting it earlier is still picked up. A company that filed nothing shows nothing.
  • The SOP call is a model, not advice. It is the same engine the simulator backtests, run on today's data. A BUY is the rule set firing, not a forecast.
  • The AI narrative is generated text. It describes levels computed from real price data, but the prose itself is a language model's and is not checked figure by figure.
Technical appendix — exact tables and endpoints
  • Prices: nepse_daily_stock_prices (raw EOD), nepse_todayprice_adj (corporate-action factors). Combined by the workbench's shared dataframe builder, 5 years back, with an unadjusted fallback.
  • Index: nepse_market_indices — used for the 5-year weekly beta and the SOP regime filter (NEPSE 200-SMA).
  • Financials: fundamentals_financialstatdbs, served by /fundamentals/api/ and /fundamentals/api/matrix/.
  • Synced snapshot: FundaFundamentalSnapshot, filled by /stock/api/funda/sync/ from funda.aurasrp.com.np (signed-in users only — it triggers an outbound fetch and a write).
  • Proposed dividends: proposed_dividends.
  • Mutual fund NAV: mutual_fund_nav, filled by Sync Fund NAV in the Raw Inventory Manager from ShareSansar's /mutual-fund-navs feed. One row per fund per Nepali month; the feed exposes only each fund's latest reading, so history accumulates from syncs and cannot be backfilled.
  • Floorsheet: floorsheet_raw — one row per executed trade, carrying both the buying and the selling broker.
  • Beta: weekly returns vs the NEPSE Index over 5 years. Weekly, not daily — do not compare it against a daily volatility figure.
  • Returns windows use approximate NEPSE session counts, deliberately conservative: 1W = 5, 1M = 22, 3M = 66, 1Y = 246 sessions.
  • AI model: Gemini 2.5 Pro, called only when you press Generate.
