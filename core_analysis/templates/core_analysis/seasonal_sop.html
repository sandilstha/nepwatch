Seasonal Strategy Desk SOP

Methodology note for the Seasonal Strategy — Accumulation / Distribution card on the Seasonal Return desk (Workbench → Seasonal Return tab). For every NEPSE index it searches the historical calendar for the single seasonal buy-and-hold window that has paid best, then reports where to accumulate, where to distribute, and how much confidence the pattern earns. It is a rules-based, in-sample seasonal screen — not investment advice, and not a forecast.

1. What the desk does

The strategy engine turns the raw seasonality matrix into one actionable call per index. For each index it:

  • Builds a per-month signal from history (see §3).
  • Searches every possible seasonal holding window and keeps the single optimal one (see §4).
  • Reports that window as an Accumulate leg and an Exit leg, with the realistic Expected Return, the hindsight Opp ceiling, a hit rate, and a Confidence grade.
  • Ranks all indices by the Blend metric and writes a plain-English Market Outlook plus a phase chip (Accumulation / Hold / Distribution / Wait).
"Seasonal" here means a few months, not buy-and-hold: the search is capped at a 7-month maximum window (_MAX_HOLD).

2. Data & inputs

  • Source: the locally-synced NEPSE index history (NepseMarketIndex) — the NEPSE Index and its sub-indices. No external calls.
  • Unit of return: month-over-month change of each index's month-end close, grouped by calendar month across all available years.
  • Calendar: the maths are Gregorian; rows are displayed in Nepali fiscal-year order (Shrawan → Ashadh), and Bikram Sambat labels map each Gregorian month to the BS month that begins within it (approximate).
  • Current month excluded: the in-progress month is never counted, so a one-day partial print can't skew a season.
  • Gaps skipped: a year missing any month inside a candidate window is dropped for that window — you can't trade through a gap you don't have data for.

3. The three signals

Every calculation runs on three per-month series:

SignalWhat it isRole
Return % (realistic)The actual, bookable month-over-month close return.The number you can actually earn — reported as Expected Return %.
Opp % (hindsight)The best-case directional intra-month swing: from the first trading day's High down to the month Low when the high printed first, else from that day's Low up to the month High. opp = (target − ref) / ref × 100.A ceiling that assumes perfect timing — not repeatable.
BlendThe per-month average of the two: blend = (Return% + Opp%) / 2.The signal the window search optimises and the table ranks by.
If an index has no Opp history the blend falls back to the pure Return signal, so young/thin indices still get a call where they can support one.

4. How the window is chosen

For each index the engine tries every start month s (Jan…Dec) and every length L from 1 to 7 months. For each (s, L):

  • Compound it year by year. For every year that has month s, multiply the blend returns of the L consecutive months: ∏(1 + rₖ/100) − 1. Skip any year missing a month in that window.
  • Score it. The score is the outlier-trimmed mean of those yearly compounded returns (drop the best and worst year once ≥ 6 years exist).
  • Rank candidates by (score, hit-rate, shorter hold) — highest trimmed return first, ties broken by how often it paid, then by the shorter hold (more capital-efficient).

The winning (s, L) becomes the recommendation, provided it cleared ≥ 5 years of history. The engine then re-computes each leg on that same window: Expected Return % from the real returns, Opp % from the opp returns, and the Blend used to rank.

Stability guard (split-half)

With ≥ 6 years, the window's realised real returns are split into two chronological halves. If the average wasn't positive in both halves, the pattern only worked in one era of the history (a winner's-curse risk from the wide search) and its Confidence grade is downgraded one notch.

If the best window's Expected Return % is ≤ 0 — i.e. the blend was carried by the Opp ceiling, not a real edge — the desk refuses a seasonal long and marks the row "range only (see Opp %); avoid a seasonal long."

6. Entry, exit & week timing

From the chosen (s, L) the engine derives the legs:

  • Entry month = the month before the first strong month s — you buy into weakness at its close.
  • Exit month = the last captured month of the window — you sell into strength at its close.

Where enough history exists (≥ 3 sessions/month), the desk also reports where in the month the entry month's low and the exit month's high have historically printed, as early / mid / late:

  • Position 0–0.35 of the month's sessions → early; 0.35–0.65 → mid; > 0.65 → late.
  • This refines "Accumulate Mar" into "buy around mid-Mar", and "Exit Jul" into "sell around early-Jul". A tendency, not a rule.

7. Market outlook & phase

The phase chip and the outlook paragraph are anchored on the NEPSE Index window versus the current month:

PhaseWhenStance
AccumulationThe NEPSE strong window opens within ~2 months.Start building positions into the entry month.
HoldThe current month is inside the strong window.Hold; plan to distribute into strength at the exit.
DistributionCurrent month is the exit month or the one after.Reduce / exit as the strong window closes.
WaitBetween the exit and the next entry.Seasonally soft — stay patient until the next window.

The Top sectors line quotes the highest-Blend investable sectors for the upcoming horizon — the envelope (earliest upcoming entry → latest exit) of the top-3 windows, so every named sector actually falls inside the stated date range. NEPSE Index and Mutual Fund are kept in the table but excluded from the "top sectors" ranking (they aren't investable sectors). Rows with no positive real edge, or too little history, sink to the bottom and are marked non-rankable.

8. Operating steps

  • Open Workbench → Seasonal Return; expand the Seasonal Strategy — Accumulation / Distribution card with its ? toggle.
  • Read the phase chip and Market Outlook for the top-down seasonal stance and horizon.
  • Scan the table top-down (it's ranked by Blend). For each candidate, check that Expected Return % is meaningfully positive — don't trade a row carried only by Opp %.
  • Prefer High / Medium confidence with a strong Hit Rate and several Yrs of history.
  • Use the Accumulate / Exit months and the early/mid/late timing to place the legs; size to the confidence, not the headline number.

9. Limits

This is an in-sample seasonal screen: the optimal window is found by searching the same history it is scored on, so real out-of-sample returns are typically lower than the headline. Expected Return % is a trimmed historical average, not a promise; Opp % is a perfect-timing ceiling you cannot book in advance. Seasonality can and does break — regime shifts, thin/illiquid history, and structural changes all erode it. Nepali (BS) month labels are approximate because BS months straddle two Gregorian months, while the averages themselves stay Gregorian.

A descriptive timing aid, not investment advice. It does not forecast price or replace risk management and position sizing.
