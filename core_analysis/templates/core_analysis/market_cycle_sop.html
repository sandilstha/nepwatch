NEPSE Market Cycle Stage Classification — SOP

A repeatable weekly procedure to classify NEPSE's current market stage (1–4) and flag transitions early, replacing simple "bullish / bearish" labelling. It reads the whole index top-down — distinct from the per-symbol Stage Analysis desk.

1. Objective

Determine, at any point in time, which of the four Weinstein stages NEPSE is in:

StageNameCharacter
Stage 1Accumulation / BaseSideways, low volatility; price crosses the 30-week MA back and forth.
Stage 2Advancing / BullHigher highs & higher lows; price > a rising MA; expanding breadth.
Stage 3Distribution / TopSideways after an uptrend; breadth narrows; volatility rises.
Stage 4Declining / BearLower highs & lower lows; price < a falling MA; broad selling.

2. Required data inputs

Collect weekly, ideally from the NEPSE official site, Merolagani, NepaliPaisa, or ShareSansaar:

  • NEPSE Index — daily close (last 52 weeks minimum).
  • 30-week (or 150-day) and 50-day / 200-day moving averages.
  • Weekly trading turnover (NRs).
  • Market breadth: advancing vs. declining stocks per session.
  • Sectoral index performance (Banking, Hydropower, Finance, Microfinance, …).
  • New 52-week highs vs. new 52-week lows (count of stocks).
  • Monetary-policy / regulatory catalysts (NRB circulars, margin-lending rules, CD-ratio changes).

3. Step-by-step classification procedure

Step 1 — Plot trend structure

  • Mark the last 3 major swing highs and swing lows on the NEPSE index.
  • Higher-Highs / Higher-Lows → Stage 2 candidate. Lower-Highs / Lower-Lows → Stage 4 candidate. Sideways / overlapping → Stage 1 or 3 candidate.

Step 2 — Check moving-average posture

  • Is price above or below the 30-week MA? Is the 30-week MA sloping up, flat, or down?
  • Golden Cross (50 > 200) → supports Stage 2. Death Cross (50 < 200) → supports Stage 4.

Step 3 — Score market breadth (last 10 sessions)

  • Advancers / decliners ratio averaged over 10 sessions.
  • Consistently > 1.5 → healthy Stage 2. Near 1.0 or below despite a flat/rising index → Stage 3 warning. Consistently < 0.7 → Stage 4.

Step 4 — Check volume / turnover behaviour

  • Rising price + rising turnover → confirms Stage 2.
  • Rising or flat price + falling turnover → Stage 3 warning (distribution).
  • Falling price + rising turnover → confirms Stage 4 (capitulation / panic).
  • Falling price + falling turnover → late Stage 4 / approaching Stage 1 (exhaustion).

Step 5 — Check sector leadership

  • Are Banking + large-cap sectors still leading, or lagging speculative sectors (Hydropower, Trading, Microfinance)?
  • Large-caps / banks weakening first while small-caps still rally → classic late-Stage-2 / Stage-3 signal.

Step 6 — Count new highs vs. new lows

  • Rising index but shrinking count of new 52-week highs → non-confirmation, Stage 3 warning.
  • Rising count of new 52-week lows even while the index is flat/up → Stage 3→4 warning.

Step 7 — Assign a stage score

Score each of Steps 1–6 as +1 (bullish / Stage 2), 0 (neutral / transition), or −1 (bearish / Stage 4). Sum the score:

Total scoreClassification
+4 to +6Stage 2 (confirmed)
+1 to +3Stage 2 (late / maturing) — watch closely
0Stage 3 (Distribution) — transition zone
−1 to −3Stage 4 (early / confirmed Bear)
−4 to −6Stage 4 (mature Bear)
Near 0, sideways after prolonged Stage 4, volatility compressingStage 1 (Base building)

4. Stage transition checklists

Stage 2 → Stage 3 (Distribution)

  • Index struggles to make new highs near prior resistance.
  • Turnover declining despite flat/rising price.
  • Banking / large-caps underperforming the broader index.
  • Advance / decline ratio deteriorating over 10+ sessions.
  • Fewer stocks hitting new 52-week highs.
  • Increased single-day volatility (>1.5% swings becoming frequent).
Trigger rule: if 4 of 6 boxes checked for 2+ consecutive weeks → reclassify as Stage 3.

Stage 3 → Stage 4 (Bear confirmed)

  • Index closes below the 30-week MA on rising volume.
  • 50-day MA crosses below the 200-day MA (Death Cross).
  • Advance / decline ratio falls below 0.7 for 2+ weeks.
  • New 52-week lows outnumber new highs.
  • Broad-based selling (10+ of 13 sectoral sub-indices negative) on multiple sessions.
Trigger rule: if 3 of 5 boxes checked → reclassify as Stage 4.

Stage 4 → Stage 1 (Base forming)

  • Rate of decline slows (smaller average daily point loss).
  • Turnover shrinks to multi-month lows (capitulation exhausted).
  • Index trades sideways in a defined range for 8+ weeks.
  • Advance / decline ratio stabilises near 1.0.

Stage 1 → Stage 2 (New bull)

  • Index closes above the 30-week MA and holds for 3+ weeks.
  • 50-day MA turns up and crosses above the 200-day MA.
  • Breadth expands — advancers durably outnumber decliners.
  • Volume expands on up-days relative to down-days.

7. Dashboard automation notes

If building this into an automated dashboard:

  • Pull daily close, turnover, and advance / decline counts via API or a scraped source.
  • Compute the 30-week and 50 / 200-day MAs programmatically.
  • Auto-calculate the Step 7 score and flag when a transition checklist crosses its trigger threshold.
  • Alert (email / notification) when the stage classification changes.
