Standard Operating Procedure

The single source of truth for how every number on the Floorsheet (Dalal Street X) broker desk is computed — the formula, the inputs, the data source, the update frequency, the assumptions, and how to read it. Everything here is a factual roll-up of NEPSE's broker-tagged floorsheet; nothing is a prediction, a “smart-money” label, or investment advice.

Section 1

Purpose & scope

NEPSE publishes a floorsheet: every executed trade tagged with its buying broker and selling broker. That broker attribution is what makes this desk possible — it lets us answer who is trading what, on which side, and how persistently. The desk turns the raw trade log into seven lenses (the tabs) on broker flow, concentration and rotation.

Every metric is descriptive — a sum, a share, a net, a count or a ratio over trades that already happened. No metric forecasts price or labels a broker “right”.

Section 3

How a session is aggregated

A session is ~50k+ rows, so we never sum trade-by-trade in Python. Each day is collapsed in the database with two grouped queries (one per side), riding the (business_date, buyer) / (business_date, seller) indexes:

buy [symbol][broker] = [ Σ quantity , Σ amount ] where broker = the BUYER sell[symbol][broker] = [ Σ quantity , Σ amount ] where broker = the SELLER sector[symbol] = the stock's sector label

Each per-day aggregate is cached and reused. A multi-session window (1 week, 1 month, …) is built by merging the cached per-day aggregates, never by a wide BETWEEN … GROUP BY (which would filesort millions of rows and is ~300× slower). This is why the first load of a fresh day is slower than subsequent ones, and why widening the range stays fast.

In aggregate, total buy quantity of a stock equals its total sell quantity (every share bought was sold) — so “traded shares” for a stock is measured once, on the buy side.

Section 5

Core concepts used across tabs

Net position

net(broker, stock) = buy quantity − sell quantity ( > 0 accumulating · < 0 distributing )

When several brokers are selected, their flow is pooled (quantities summed per stock) so the tables show the combined desk.

HHI — Herfindahl–Hirschman concentration

shareᵢ = brokerᵢ's traded qty in the stock ÷ all brokers' traded qty HHI = Σ shareᵢ² × 10,000 ( 0 = fully fragmented … 10,000 = one broker is the whole stock )
LowHHI < 1,500
Moderate1,500 – 2,500
High> 2,500

Bands follow the DOJ/FTC merger-guideline thresholds, so “high” is a recognised market-structure read, not an invented one. High HHI means a stock's trading is controlled by very few brokers.

Matching & churn (two-sided flow)

matching amount = min( buy amount , sell amount ) matching % = 2 × min(buy, sell) ÷ (buy + sell) × 100 ( 100 = perfectly balanced two-siding ) churn % = 2 × min(buyQ, sellQ) ÷ (buyQ + sellQ) × 100 ( same idea, on quantity )

A high matching/churn % means the broker both bought and sold the name heavily — market-making or position-recycling rather than a one-directional bet.

Broker Flow Radar

Ranks every broker in the market by activity and directional pressure over the selected window. One row per broker, sorted by total rupee activity (biggest desks first).

ColumnFormula / meaning
Buy / Sell QtyΣ shares the broker bought / sold across the window.
Buy / Sell AmountΣ rupee value bought / sold.
Total activitybuy amount + sell amount (the ranking key — gross footprint).
Differencebuy amount − sell amount (net rupee direction).
Matchingmin(buy, sell) amount, and matching % = 2·min/(buy+sell)·100 — two-sided flow.
Bias %(buy − sell) ÷ (buy + sell) × 100 — normalised tilt, −100 (all sell) … +100 (all buy).
StanceAccumulating if bias ≥ +10%, Distributing if ≤ −10%, else Balanced.

Interpretation. Big total activity + strongly positive bias = a large desk leaning to buy; high matching % on a big total = a market-maker recycling inventory rather than taking a side.

Section 6.1

A/D Radar — stock-first Accumulation / Distribution

Every other tab is broker-first: pick a broker, see what it traded. This one is stock-first: for each scrip, is someone building a position or unloading one? It is also the only metric on this desk that is backtested rather than purely descriptive, so it is documented to a higher standard — including what it cannot do.

This section is the summary. The full methodology — backtest design, the features that were tested and rejected, robustness checks and blind spots — has its own document: A/D Radar SOP.

The score

Three features, each z-scored across every scored scrip in the same window (so the reading is “relative to the rest of the market right now”, not against a fixed threshold that would drift):

FeatureDefinitionSignMeasured effect
absorbTop-5 net buyers' net quantity ÷ total window volume++57 bps per SD (t = 4.22)
sell_hhiHerfindahl concentration of the selling side−−34 bps per SD (t = −5.55)
top1_shareShare of net buying done by the single largest broker−−47 bps per SD (t = −2.23)

score = z(absorb) − z(top1_share) − z(sell_hhi)

The two negative signs are the important part. Concentrated selling is the most statistically reliable feature on the whole desk — a seller with size has to keep hitting bids and cannot hide it, whereas a patient accumulator genuinely can spread buying across brokers and days. And conditional on how much is being absorbed, buying concentrated in one broker scores worse than the same buying spread across several: real campaigns run through multiple brokers.

Bands

Percentile of the cross-section, cut into quintiles to match how the backtest was measured: top 20% Accumulation, next 20% Mild Accumulation, middle 20% Neutral, then Mild Distribution and Distribution. A neutral reading is a real answer — no side is controlling the scrip — not a missing one.

Universe

Ordinary equities only, priced ≥ Rs 50, trading ≥ 800 shares/session over the window. Debentures, mutual funds and promoter shares are excluded: they change hands through one or two brokers, so every concentration measure pins at its extreme and would swamp both tails. Promoter scrips carry their parent's sector (CZBILP reads as “Commercial Banks”), so they are caught by symbol suffix rather than sector. The count of exclusions is printed under the table on every scan.

Backtest

3.5 years of floorsheet (2023-01-01 → 2026-08-11; 819 sessions; 9.76 M broker-day-stock cells). Features use only data up to the anchor date, forward returns start the day after. Returns are adjusted for bonus/rights — raw NEPSE closes book a 10–15% bonus ex-date as a crash — and measured as excess over the cross-sectional median of the same date, so a rising market cannot make a worthless signal look predictive. Only non-overlapping windows are reported.

HorizonBottom quintileTop quintileSpreadt-stat
20 sessions+0.18%+0.94%+0.76%—
60 sessions−0.75%+3.60%+4.34%1.96
120 sessions−0.91%+5.39%+6.30%2.24

Positive in all four years at the 120-session horizon (2023 +6.8%, 2024 +9.2%, 2025 +2.7%, 2026 +3.2%), and it is not a penny-stock artifact — the spread is largest in the most expensive price tercile (+8.0%).

How to read it — and how not to

  • It is a 3–6 month positioning screen. At 20 sessions the edge (~0.8%) does not clear the ~0.6% round trip. Traded weekly it will lose money.
  • Hit rate is ~53%. The edge is in the average across many names, not in any single call. Roughly half of top-quintile scrips still underperform.
  • 2025 was weak (+2.7%). It can go quiet for a year at a time.
  • It is a screen, not a signal — it tells you where to look, not what to buy.

Blind spot

Shares moved off-market — DP/BOD transfers, pledges, private deals — never print on the floorsheet. A campaign conducted that way is completely invisible here, and no public data source closes that gap. A “Neutral” reading means no on-market campaign was detected, which is not the same as “nothing is happening”.

7.3 Persistent Accumulation / Distribution

A multi-session lens (own lookback: 1 week / 1 month / 3 months, floored at 1 week). It walks every session in the window newest-first and, per stock, reports:

FieldDefinition
StreakConsecutive most-recent sessions the desk stayed on one side; breaks on a flat or opposite session. The current conviction run.

Streak and Net Qty are broker-specific (only broker-tagged floorsheet makes them computable); HHI is market-wide. Both are factual — no “smart money”/“success” labelling.

Signal Desk

Four research signals for the selected desk, all from one window pass. Each is a factual roll-up, never a prediction.

SignalDefinition
Price–Flow DivergenceThe desk's net flow disagrees with the stock's price move over the window: net-buying while price fell (accum-weak), or net-selling into a rising price (distrib-strong). Ranked by absolute net.
Sector RotationThe desk's net quantity (buy − sell) rolled up by sector — where it is adding vs trimming exposure. Ranked by absolute net.
Buyer / Seller BreadthMarket-wide: the count of distinct brokers net-buying vs net-selling each stock (consensus vs disagreement). Complements HHI.
Two-Sided ActivityStocks the desk both bought and sold heavily. churn = 2·min/(buy+sell)·100; shown only when churn ≥ 33 (at least a 1:2 split), ranked by the genuinely two-sided volume (the smaller side).

Divergence uses the window's first-to-last close change from the local price table alongside the broker net; every other signal is pure floorsheet.

A treemap of the selected desk's net position per stock over the window:

Broker Exposure Monitor

Per-stock top-3 broker concentration on each side — how dominated a stock's trading is:

broker % = broker's traded shares in the stock ÷ the stock's total traded shares × 100 buy_sum / sell_sum = the top-3 brokers' combined % on that side

One row per stock (sorted by total trading), showing the three biggest buyers and sellers with their percentages. A high top-3 sum means a few brokers drive that side — the same signal HHI gives, read name-by-name. A sector filter narrows the list.

Section 13 · Appendix

Interpretation guide & limitations

  • Descriptive, not predictive. Every figure summarises trades that already happened. A broker accumulating a name is not a “buy” recommendation, and the desk never scores brokers as right/wrong.
  • End-of-session only. No intraday view; the newest data is the last synced session.
  • Broker ≠ beneficial owner. One broker executes for many clients; flow is the firm's, not a single investor's. Big two-sided (high churn) desks are often market-makers, not directional bets.
  • Net position ≠ custody. “Holdings” and net figures are trade-derived over the window, not depository balances.
  • Windows merge sessions. A wider range pools more sessions; a name quiet lately can still rank on a long window from earlier activity.
  • Mutual-fund exclusion is best-effort. Keyed on the “Mutual Fund” sector, with a symbol-suffix fallback for blank-sector rows.
  • Not investment advice. The desk is a monitoring tool over public floorsheet data.
